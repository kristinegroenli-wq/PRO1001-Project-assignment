How to prevent airborne diseases

Due to effective vaccination programs, Norway has very few cases of dangerous airborne diseases such as tuberculosis and measles. But for people in high-risk groups, even a common cold could be dangerous. That's why it's important for the population as whole to try to prevent the spread of these diseases. Airborne diseases spread through drops of saliva, water, aerosoles or dust particles. This page will provide you with information on how to prevent spreading

Vaccination

Vaccines are used for protecting the people in high-risk groups. This includes influenza and pneumokokk vaccination of elderly and pregnant people, and people with imunesupressive diseases

Hygiene

Handwash with soap and water can prevent multiple infections. Good cough hygiene is also important, like sneezing in a napkin or in your elbow instead of in your hands

Facemask and distance

If you are coughing, try reducing the risk of spreading disease by keeping a distance from other people, or use a face mask

Ventilation and water safety

As airborne diseases spreads more easely indoors, it's important to have good ventilation. It is also important to not let warm water stay too long in pipes, as this creates an environment for Legionella to thrive
